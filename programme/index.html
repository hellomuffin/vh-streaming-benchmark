The household assistant programme

A simulated person does a chore in a rendered house while an AI assistant watches through a camera and may speak. The engine injects mistakes into the person's plan. The assistant is scored on noticing each one, naming it correctly, guiding the repair, and staying quiet when nothing is wrong. This is the complete record: the environment, the tasks, the error taxonomy, two benchmarks built on them, and the results of running five assistants through the harder one.

1 · The environment

The world is VirtualHome apartment 2, read as the engine's native scene graph: typed object nodes with ids, states and ON/INSIDE relation edges, plus a body node. The scene holds 183 objects across a kitchen, a living room and two bedrooms — and, deliberately, duplicates: nine plates, seven books, three table lamps. Duplication is what makes which one a real question, and therefore what makes wrong-object errors measurable.

The symbolic twin

Live Unity is slow and non-deterministic, so it cannot dry-run cases or tell a bug from a model. A deterministic twin over the same graph replays a plan in milliseconds and pre-verifies every case. Keeping the two in agreement is most of the engineering, and is done by four mechanisms rather than trust: the twin is seeded from the rendered graph rather than replayed; every manipulation's postcondition is checked against the expected graph delta; every frame must have been captured from the body's current pose; and a movement command that reports success without moving is refused honestly.

Time, and the progress key

One tick is one second, rendered as eight frames. Task progress is carried by a partial state — what is held, where the tracked objects sit, what is open, what is on — restricted to the classes the plan touches, with position and duration dropped. That key is order-invariant, which is what lets a single injection anchor fire correctly however the person sequences the chore.

What each agent sees

The person gets its own camera, proprioception (hands, queue, position, the outcome of each action), its own past-action log, and two maps: the current room's scene graph with every device's live state, and a whole-house overview with the door graph and every room that still has something on. The assistant gets eight first-person frames per turn, the same two maps, a one-line body ground-truth, its own previous reply as its only memory, and the conversation. It never sees object ids, coordinates, the grading machinery, or the person's private channel — asserted on the composed prompt at run time, not on the template.

2 · The tasks

19 episodes over 10 families. A task is specified by an instruction, a reference plan that replays to success headlessly, and named checkpoints that latch on resolved events — the object classes and instances the gated controller actually manipulated, never the words the model typed. Hard episodes traverse three or more rooms. Instructions are precise but minimal: an object is qualified only when its class has confusable instances, and the route is never spelled out.

3 · The error taxonomy

Errors are typed transforms on the subgoal graph, re-categorised so every class has a unique observable signature; timing and scope are parameters, never separate classes. Two labels are twins when the deviation is indistinguishable between them in the video channel at the decisive moment — an in-window twin name is credited, because an assistant that reads the deviation right under a defensible label has detected it.

labeldefinition given to both agentscredited twins
wrong_objectthe object in hand/use is a look-alike of the required one (e.g. the juice instead of the milk); the plan's structure stays otherwise correct—
wrong_placementthe right object is set down in the WRONG place — the destination the task called for never receives it, and she treats the job as finished therewrong_object
navigation_inefficiencyextra movement with ZERO state change (multi-room detour or hanging around a room), then the plan resumes intactskipped_step, stall
skipped_stepa required step is OMITTED ENTIRELY — she never goes to its target at all, simply carrying on with the next thing, and the rest runs in order with the omission's visible consequencesmissing_step, navigation_inefficiency
repetitionshe goes back to something she has ALREADY FINISHED and does it again. On a switch, a door or any toggle the second press INVERTS what she achieved — the lamp she turned off is lit again, the fridge she shut is open again — so the tell is that she REVISITED a finished target, not that the end state is 'done twice'. On a placement she takes the object out and puts it back.navigation_inefficiency
stallthey stop moving mid-task — stationary, nothing happening (may or may not resume)navigation_inefficiency
missing_stepa goal that covers SEVERAL identical objects is left half done — the same action was carried out on one instance and its twin was forgotten (one lamp switched off, the other left on); differs from skipped_step, where a whole distinct step is omitted in passingskipped_step

Classes indistinguishable in video were cut at re-categorisation. The safety family — leave a door open, light a device and leave — exists in the transform library but is absent from the graded taxonomy and the scorer: VirtualHome has no consequence dynamics, so "left the stove on" is visually identical to "turned the stove on", and a metric for an outcome the engine cannot generate is a permanent zero that looks like a result.

5 · Benchmark B — the composite suite

The single-error suite asks whether an assistant can catch one thing going wrong. The composite suite asks what the single-error setting cannot: whether it can hold a running model of the task while more than one thing goes wrong — notice a second mistake after committing to a first, avoid attributing both to one cause, and keep the repair of one from masking the other. When two errors may share an episode

Errors that collide measure nothing. Eight rules, each checked mechanically per episode; the ledger records every verdict and every rejection's reason.

rulenamewhy
R1Direct-twin exclusionNo error may be an observation-channel twin of another in the same episode. Twins are the labels the grader already credits for one another, so a correct flag for one would be creditable for both and the episode could not say which the assistant saw.
R2Anchor survivalEvery declared error must survive composition. Transforms apply in order and a later error whose anchor an earlier one removed is skipped-with-record — which forbids anchoring anything after a truncating error, or on a subgoal an omission deletes.
R3Window separationThe errors' anchors must be distinct and separated by at least one surviving subgoal, so their detection windows do not touch and each flag attributes to exactly one error.
R4Independent observabilityNo two errors may act on the same object, and an earlier error must not make a later one unobservable — skipping a fetch makes a later misplacement of that object impossible.
R5Semantic plausibilityThe combination must read as one person having a plausibly bad run rather than two unrelated glitches. The one rule a replay cannot check, so it is audited per episode by a VLM and by independent readers.
R6Visible stateA repetition's inverting press must change the picture. A microwave, dishwasher or stove switch renders identically to not pressing it, so a repetition there has no evidence on camera; lamps, the TV and container doors do.
R7The misplacement must move somethingA wrong placement may not put the object on the same kind of surface it started on — a lift-and-replace has no state change and reads as a pointless detour.
R8Per-phase distinctivenessEach error separately must add a command the clean plan lacks (the inaction classes excepted). Checking only the whole composed plan let one error supply the distinctive commands while another added nothing and was indistinguishable from ordinary execution.

A structural result. Under the corrected separation rule, every two- and three-subgoal task fails: there is nowhere to put a second error with a surviving subgoal between it and the first. Multi-error work lives in tasks of four or more subgoals, and only the eight-subgoal routine hosts three.

How each episode was verified

Three passes that did not share verdicts, so agreement is evidence rather than an echo: a mechanical dry-replay through the twin (12/12 admitted); a VLM audit of each episode alone, on executability, whether every error is observable, whether a watcher could attribute each correctly, and plausibility; and three independent readers given the engine sources and told to be sceptical. The readers found the most, and every finding was a real defect — several engine-wide rather than specific to this suite.

6 · Making a multi-error episode runnable

The plan layer always composed several errors correctly; the rollout did not, and the reason was structural. It modelled an error as a divergence of the whole plan, not as an event. One prefix diff gave exactly one deviation point, so two errors fused into a single deviation, a single tasked sequence and a single takeover; the takeover was a one-shot latch; and recovery was defined as clearing the queue, which deleted the second error the moment the assistant succeeded on the first.

The fix changes the unit from the plan diverged to an error happened. Phase k is the single-error problem taken against the baseline of every earlier error having already happened — build the plan with the first k calls and with the first k+1, and diff those two:

phase 0  skipped_step   m=0   tasked: walk to the book, grab the book, ...
phase 1  wrong_object   m=6   tasked: grab the juice, close the fridge, ...
phase 2  stall          m=15  tasked: stand still

The rollout's existing single-error state cells are the current phase; a phase advance saves them back and loads the next in, so a single-error case is exactly one phase and the shipped path is unchanged. Release hands the chore back, re-bases the next trigger deadline, and re-queues the remaining steps so the person carries on and can reach the next anchor. Grading needed no new machinery: the verdict function was already a pure function of one error, window and deviation tick, so it is called once per fired phase and the flag is credited to the phase that grades it best — which dissolves twin-laundering by construction, since naming the second error exactly outranks the alias it would earn against a twin first error.

Verified live: in cx_routine_skip_obj phase 0 injected at t0 and released at t12, and phase 1 injected at t20 and released at t22 — each with its own trigger, tasked sequence and release. A single-error control ran alongside and behaved as before.

7 · Benchmark C — the assistant sweep

Every composite episode × three personas × five assistants: 180 scored rollouts. Only the assistant varies between arms; the simulated person stays on the reference model throughout, because it is the environment rather than the system under test.

The three personas

The first two vary ability and receptiveness together — the novice is unskilled and receptive, the expert skilled and closed — so any result could be explained by either dial. The third is skilled and genuinely receptive, which is the cell that separates them.

Harness

The assistant is swapped by environment variable, with per-model sampling profiles (the shape a model requires, not a tuning knob), three retries and then an empty reply — the harness never repairs a model's output, because a repaired reply measures the repair. Per-call token and latency accounting is stamped on every report. Local models are served on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint that accepts image parts.

9 · Findings

The rankings are one result. These are the durable ones — each was a real defect, found by audit or by the sweep, and each generalises past this engine.

The flag-shape trap

assistant sweep

Qwen3-VL first scored a flat zero on every detection metric. It emitted 115 of its 118 flags as a bare string ("flag": "wrong_placement") where the harness accepted only {"error": …}, so every genuine detection was discarded. Regraded from the models' own replies it has the highest precision in the sweep, at 3.6% schema compliance. Gemini Flash shows the same failure milder (71%). A harness that accepts only its declared schema scores formatting failures as perception failures — and would have published "open-weight models cannot detect errors at all".

Definitions must describe the realization

taxonomy

Three graded labels described behaviour the engine had stopped producing: repetition said "a state change happens twice" while the realization inverts a toggle; skipped_step said "walks right past its target" after omissions stopped navigating; wrong_placement promised a later device running empty after the error was made to commit at the placement. An assistant told to look for evidence that never appears will mislabel a correct perception.

Composition breaks transforms written for one error

engine

wrong_placement truncated the plan at the first injected node it found. With any earlier error's node present it deleted the rest of the plan including its own placement, while both records reported success — one pair composed down to a single command.

Grade offline, from what was recorded

grading

The live rollout closed a detection window when the next error began; the contract runs a window to end of episode. Closing early scored a correct-but-late twin detection as a miss. Grading now recomputes from stored flag ticks, which also means a grading rule can be corrected without re-running a single episode.

An error's evidence must exist on some channel

engine

A forgotten fridge door was unobservable on every channel at once: the person never returns to it, the frames never show it, and the whole-house map reported only devices left ON. The map now reports open containers too.

Decoys must be deliberate

scene

routine_hard never darkened the scene's other two lit lamps, so an assistant could flag "a lamp is still on" without ever observing the injected omission. A second milk beside the microwave was likewise what the nearest-instance binder grabbed during a legitimate repair.

Short tasks cannot host two attributable errors

benchmark design

Under the corrected separation rule every two- and three-subgoal task fails: there is nowhere to put a second error with a surviving subgoal between. Multi-error work lives in tasks of four or more subgoals, and only the eight-subgoal routine hosts three.

The bottleneck was rendering, not compute

operations

Episodes are 12–20 minutes of software-GL rendering. The node has 243 cores; going from 4 engines to 16 took a 180-cell sweep from roughly ten hours to about four, with the GPUs untouched by the simulator.

10 · Limits, stated

  • Three metrics are not measured, and are reported as such rather than as zeros: answer accuracy (these chores ask the assistant no scored questions), alert score (no standing "tell me when it's done" request), and the fifteen-item assistance-quality rubric (its judge was not run).
  • Substitutions. No OpenAI key existed in this environment, so every GPT arm was impossible. The reference spec's flash tier was not offered on the available key; a sibling stands in. The spec's three local models are not on this filesystem; two open-weight VLMs that are were served instead.
  • One arm's task metrics are not comparable. Because the live harness did not recognise Qwen3-VL's flags, its takeover never released on detection, so its success and efficiency figures understate it. Its detection figures are fair, having been regraded from what it actually said.
  • One seed per cell. No confidence intervals are reported, matching the reference programme, which reports reliability through cross-model agreement instead.
  • An omission in an order-free task is only strictly true at the end. Its window opens on the successor subgoal, at which moment the person could still return to the target. The assistant is therefore reasoning about progress, not reading a committed fact — an honest asymmetry with the other classes.
