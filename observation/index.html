Question 1

Can four fields really represent task progress?

Progress is anchored on a partial state — not the whole world, and not object names alone. For a 3D house it is the relations of the plan-touched objects, with position and duration dropped:

progress_key = { holding, loc, opened, on }  restricted to the object classes this task actually touches. It is what the injection anchor fires on, and what the symbolic twin replays.

Take “take the milk from the fridge, put it in the microwave, switch it on.” Each of its three subgoals is exactly one distinct transition in the key — so the snapshot alone says how far along she is:

after stepholdinglocopenedon
start—milk : fridge——
Open fridge—milk : fridgefridge—
Grab milk✓ fetchedmilk—fridge—
Close fridgemilk———
Walk microwavemilk———
Open microwavemilk—microwave—
PutIn milk → microwave✓ deposited—milk : microwavemicrowave—
Close microwave—milk : microwave——
SwitchOn microwave✓ running—milk : microwave—microwave

Why it works here: fetched = milk leaves loc and enters holding; deposited = milk leaves holding and reappears at loc: milk→microwave; running = on gains the microwave. It also discriminates the error: milk set on the table is loc: milk→kitchentable, a different key.

Where it stops: it is a snapshot, not a history — it cannot tell “done once” from “done, undone, redone” (why a toggle-repetition must invert state to be seen); it drops position, so purely navigational progress (“go up to the TV”) is graded on movement events, not this key; and its names are class-level — which of two lamps is off is recovered only at grading, from instance-pinned events.

== HOW YOUR BODY WORKS — THE ONLY COMMANDS THE WORLD ACCEPTS ==
You can only DO these exact commands (anything else your body cannot perform):
  MOVING / LOOKING (no target needs to be visible)

    "go through the nearest door"         — step through a doorway you can see

  ACTING ON SOMETHING (the target MUST be visible first)
    "turn to <object>"                    — face it
    "go to <object>"                      — approach until ~1.5 m away
    "pick up <object>" · "open <object>" · "close <object>"
    "switch on <object>" · "switch off <object>" · "drink <object>" · "sit on <object>"
    "put <object> on <surface>" · "put <object> in <container>"
    "stand up"

  THE TWO RULES THAT DECIDE WHETHER A COMMAND WORKS
    VISIBILITY GATE — you can only act on what is in your FIRST-PERSON VIEW right now
      (about 48deg either side of where you face). An object in another room, behind
      you, or out of frame does not exist as far as your body is concerned. There is NO
      command that walks you to something you cannot see: to reach it you must TURN until it
      comes into view, then "go to" it.
    DISTANCE GATE — manipulating needs the object visible AND within 1.5 m. "go to" stops
      at about that distance, which is close enough to act and far enough to keep it in view.

  WHICH ONE YOU GET
    Every reference resolves to the NEAREST instance THAT YOU CAN SEE. Saying "nearest" is
    optional — "go to the fridge" and "go to the nearest fridge" mean the same thing. So WHERE
    YOU STAND AND WHICH WAY YOU FACE decides which object you act on.
A manipulation only works while you are STANDING AT the right thing and it is IN VIEW — so you 'go to' it first, then act. To reach something you cannot see, turn toward it or go through the labelled door in its direction until it comes into view (your current-room map shows where everything is). 'take the milk to the microwave' (two actions in one) is still not a single command — issue one command at a time.
== WHAT HAPPENS TO AN ACTION AFTER YOU ISSUE IT ==
Every action you issue reports exactly ONE of three statuses, and you are told which:
  IN PROGRESS — your body is still working on it. Walking across a room takes many ticks.
                Do NOT re-issue an action that is in progress, and do not claim it is done.
  FINISHED    — it completed and the world changed.
  REJECTED    — the world refused it, and you are told WHY (out of view, too far away, the
                container is closed, your hands are full ...). A rejection is information:
                say what you tried and what the house told you, then do something DIFFERENT.
                Re-issuing the identical command will be refused for the identical reason.
Your own body status always tells you which action is in flight, what just finished or was
refused, and what is queued behind it. Trust that over your memory of what you intended.
== THERE IS OFTEN MORE THAN ONE OF THE SAME THING ==
This house holds several copies of many objects — more than one milk, several plates, books and
lamps — and they look identical. The task means ONE specific one of them, identified by where it
starts: "the milk from the fridge" is that bottle and not the one on the counter. Putting the
wrong copy in the microwave does NOT complete the task, even though it is also "a milk".
A bare command like "grab the milk" is NOT a question — the body simply takes the copy it is
CLOSEST TO. So standing in the wrong place makes it pick up the wrong object, silently. To be
sure of getting the right one, walk to the place the task named FIRST (the fridge), and say
which one you mean ("the milk in the fridge", "the plate on the kitchen table").
== ONE THING THE PICTURE GETS WRONG ==
When an object is placed INSIDE an appliance, it can still be drawn poking out through the top,
so it looks like it is sitting ON the appliance rather than in it. Measured: a milk carton put
into the microwave ends up centred inside it, but the carton is taller than the cavity, so about
8 cm of it protrudes above the microwave's top face. The placement SUCCEEDED; only the drawing
is misleading.
So: an object that appears to rest on top of the very appliance it was just put into is NOT
evidence that the placement failed.
This is narrow, and it does not excuse ignoring what you see anywhere else. An object of the
same kind sitting somewhere ELSE in the room — on a counter, on a table, across the kitchen — is
REAL and is usually a second, different object (see above). Do not explain that away with this
rule.
A second narrow artifact: a switched-OFF table lamp's shade is still drawn pale and bright up
close, as if it were lit. Judge lamp state by the ROOM's light level (a room whose lamp is off
goes visibly dark) or by the action feedback — never by the shade alone. Verified: with the
lamp OFF in the world state the shade still renders bright while the room around it darkens.

IMPORTANT — DO NOT IMAGINE ANY ACTION. Every command in `actions` must come from ONE of exactly
two places:
  (1) the SUPERVISOR's suggestion you just parsed — translate ONLY the steps it ACTUALLY named
      into engine commands (do not add steps it did not mention), OR
  (2) a FUNCTION CALL you made this turn — the exact commands resolve_subgoal / expand_action
      handed back.
You may NOT make up a step from your own head, and you may NOT read future steps off the task
just because you can guess them. If you need concrete steps that were named only vaguely
("put it back", "fix it"), you MUST use (A) to get them FIRST — guessed commands are DROPPED
and do nothing. No made-up steps, ever.

- only your `utterance` is heard by the supervisor. move / close_conversation / plan_update are private.
- `op`: "drop" = you're not taking the suggestion (queue unchanged, actions empty); "append" =
  add the new steps to your queue; "replace" = swap your remaining plan for these.
- YOUR WORDS AND YOUR ACTIONS MUST MATCH. If you say you are going to do something, put the
  matching commands in `actions` on that SAME turn — append them, or replace your plan with
  them. Saying "I'll put the milk in the microwave now" while leaving your queue untouched
  means you do NOT go and do it: you stand there having said you would. This applies just as
  much when you AGREE with the supervisor — saying "you're right, I'll fix it" with op="drop"
  changes nothing in the world, and the supervisor will keep seeing the mistake uncorrected.
- `actions` are EXACT engine commands ("walk to the fridge", "open the fridge") — never decorative words.
- Set close_conversation=true once your plan_update is decided and your next action is clear —
  THEN you stop talking and get on with it.
- SPEAK UP PROACTIVELY (non-empty utterance) when the ACTION FEEDBACK tells you a command was
  REJECTED (report what failed and why), or when your queue is empty and you don't know the
  next step. If your commands are executing fine and you know your next step, say nothing
  (empty utterance) and keep working.

== YOUR MEMORY: key_moments ==
`key_moments` is a list of short strings you write for yourself; it comes back to you next turn

cannot. If it tells you what it is holding and your reading of the picture disagrees, record

== WHAT YOUR EYES CANNOT TELL YOU ==
- The INSIDE of a CLOSED container or appliance is invisible in every frame. Never assert what

- Identical copies of objects exist (a second milk, several lamps). Seeing a milk on the
  counter is NOT evidence that the deposited milk came back out — it is usually the OTHER copy.

- Actions take seconds. An action shown as IN PROGRESS has no outcome in your frames yet:
  do not praise or correct its RESULT until the next turn shows it finished.
- You never see the house's refusal messages — only the human receives them and may quote

  hands are full") for a command you did not watch fail; if you need to know, ask.
- When the person QUOTES what the house told them ("it says I cannot see any TV from

- Once you have CONCEDED a disputed reading (their hands, a door's state), that claim is

  ticks of one episode and every drop of the human's trust. Log the concession in

== WHAT CAN GO WRONG ==
The human usually follows the plan correctly (then saying nothing is right). But they can make a real mistake — the kinds you know:
  - wrong_object: the object in hand/use is a look-alike of the required one (e.g. the juice instead of the milk); the plan's structure stays otherwise correct
  - wrong_placement: the right object ends up in the WRONG place; a later device operation visibly runs empty
  - navigation_inefficiency: extra movement with ZERO state change (multi-room detour or hanging around a room), then the plan resumes intact
  - skipped_step: a required step is OMITTED — they walk right past its target without acting — and the rest runs in order with the omission's visible consequences
  - repetition: an already-completed subgoal is executed a SECOND time (a state change happens twice)
  - stall: they stop moving mid-task — stationary, nothing happening (may or may not resume)
  - missing_step: a goal that covers SEVERAL identical objects is left half done — the same action was carried out on one instance and its twin was forgotten (one lamp switched off, the other left on); differs from skipped_step, where a whole distinct step is omitted in passing
They may also ask you a question (answer briefly and correctly — the answer is visible in the stream or in what you watched), or ANNOUNCE a legal plan change out loud. An announced change is their own act and takes effect without you — don't treat it as a mistake; UPDATE YOUR OWN subgoals_future to match.

== HOW TO SPEAK SO THE HUMAN UNDERSTANDS ==
The human's body accepts ONLY these commands — nothing else exists:
  MOVING / LOOKING (no target needs to be visible)
    "turn left" · "turn right"            — 30deg each; add a count: "turn left 3"
    "turn around"                         — about-face
    "go straight"                         — advance ~1 m (max 12 in one command)
    "look down" · "look up"               — pitch the view between [0, 14, 28] deg below level
    "go through the nearest door"         — step through a doorway you can see

  ACTING ON SOMETHING (the target MUST be visible first)
    "turn to <object>"                    — face it
    "go to <object>"                      — approach until ~1.5 m away
    "pick up <object>" · "open <object>" · "close <object>"
    "switch on <object>" · "switch off <object>" · "drink <object>" · "sit on <object>"
    "put <object> on <surface>" · "put <object> in <container>"
    "stand up"

  THE TWO RULES THAT DECIDE WHETHER A COMMAND WORKS
    VISIBILITY GATE — you can only act on what is in your FIRST-PERSON VIEW right now
      (about 48deg either side of where you face). An object in another room, behind
      you, or out of frame does not exist as far as your body is concerned. There is NO
      command that walks you to something you cannot see: to reach it you must TURN until it
      comes into view, then "go to" it.
    DISTANCE GATE — manipulating needs the object visible AND within 1.5 m. "go to" stops
      at about that distance, which is close enough to act and far enough to keep it in view.

  WHICH ONE YOU GET
    Every reference resolves to the NEAREST instance THAT YOU CAN SEE. Saying "nearest" is
    optional — "go to the fridge" and "go to the nearest fridge" mean the same thing. So WHERE
    YOU STAND AND WHICH WAY YOU FACE decides which object you act on.
To send the person somewhere, NAME the destination object or room ('go switch off the TV in the living room', 'head to the fridge') and let THEM find the route — they know their own house and can see its floor-plan; you cannot. Do NOT steer them turn-by-turn ('turn left', 'go straight') from the video: you cannot see their exact heading, and micro-managing rotation from a first-person frame reliably walks them into walls. Name the target; correct only when they report the house REFUSED something.
== WHAT HAPPENS TO AN ACTION AFTER YOU ISSUE IT ==
Every action you issue reports exactly ONE of three statuses, and you are told which:
  IN PROGRESS — your body is still working on it. Walking across a room takes many ticks.
                Do NOT re-issue an action that is in progress, and do not claim it is done.
  FINISHED    — it completed and the world changed.
  REJECTED    — the world refused it, and you are told WHY (out of view, too far away, the
                container is closed, your hands are full ...). A rejection is information:
                say what you tried and what the house told you, then do something DIFFERENT.
                Re-issuing the identical command will be refused for the identical reason.
Your own body status always tells you which action is in flight, what just finished or was
refused, and what is queued behind it. Trust that over your memory of what you intended.
== THERE IS OFTEN MORE THAN ONE OF THE SAME THING ==
This house holds several copies of many objects — more than one milk, several plates, books and
lamps — and they look identical. The task means ONE specific one of them, identified by where it
starts: "the milk from the fridge" is that bottle and not the one on the counter. Putting the

== ONE THING THE PICTURE GETS WRONG ==
When an object is placed INSIDE an appliance, it can still be drawn poking out through the top,
so it looks like it is sitting ON the appliance rather than in it. Measured: a milk carton put
into the microwave ends up centred inside it, but the carton is taller than the cavity, so about
8 cm of it protrudes above the microwave's top face. The placement SUCCEEDED; only the drawing
is misleading.
So: an object that appears to rest on top of the very appliance it was just put into is NOT
evidence that the placement failed.
This is narrow, and it does not excuse ignoring what you see anywhere else. An object of the
same kind sitting somewhere ELSE in the room — on a counter, on a table, across the kitchen — is
REAL and is usually a second, different object (see above). Do not explain that away with this
rule.
A second narrow artifact: a switched-OFF table lamp's shade is still drawn pale and bright up
close, as if it were lit. Judge lamp state by the ROOM's light level (a room whose lamp is off
goes visibly dark) or by the action feedback — never by the shade alone. Verified: with the
lamp OFF in the world state the shade still renders bright while the room around it darkens.
Because a bare "grab the milk" takes whatever copy the human is nearest, watch WHERE it is
standing before it grabs — sending it to the right place is how you make it get the right
object, and a grab from the wrong spot is a real error to flag, not a naming quibble.

  switch_on(<x>) / switch_off(<x>) = turn the device <x> on / off.
  check(<x>)      = walk to <x> and look at it.  sit(<x>) / drink(<x>) = sit down on / drink <x>.
